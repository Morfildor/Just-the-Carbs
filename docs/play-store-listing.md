## Full description

```
Just the Carbs does one thing: it tells you how many grams of carbohydrate are in the
portion of packaged food you are about to eat.

Scan the barcode. Type how much you are having. Read the number. That's it.

HOW IT WORKS

• Scan a barcode and Just the Carbs looks the product up
• Enter your portion in grams or millilitres — or, when a trustworthy per-item
  weight exists, just say how many: "2 slices" works without a kitchen scale
• The carbohydrate amount updates as you type — there is no Calculate button
• Copy the number with one tap if you need it somewhere else

BUILT FOR SPEED

Products you have used before appear on the home screen with the portion you last
used, so a repeat product takes one tap. Star the ones you use most and they stay
at the top.

YOU CAN CORRECT THE DATA

Product databases are not always right. Just the Carbs shows you where a value came
from, and lets you check it against the package and save your own. Once you have
verified a value, it is yours — the app will never quietly replace it with an
online one.

If a barcode isn't recognised, you can scan the nutrition label or type the value
in yourself. Just the Carbs never leaves you stuck.

WORKS WITHOUT A SIGNAL

Products you have used before work completely offline. Only a barcode Just the Carbs
has never seen needs an internet connection.

PRIVATE BY DEFAULT

No account. No advertising. Your products, portions and verified values stay on
your device. Just the Carbs sends an uncached barcode or a name you search for to Open
Food Facts, and loads a product photo from its allowlisted image hosts when one
exists. Google ML Kit processes camera input on-device and collects diagnostic
and usage metrics described in the privacy policy.

WHAT JUST THE CARBS DOES NOT DO

• It does not calculate insulin or any other medication
• It does not read or interpret glucose
• It does not connect to pumps or monitors
• It is not a diet tracker: no calories, no macros, no food diary, no daily totals

Just the Carbs gives you a carbohydrate number and nothing more.

Always check the package if a value looks wrong. Product recipes change, and
public food databases can be out of date.

Product data from Open Food Facts, used under the Open Database License (ODbL).
```
